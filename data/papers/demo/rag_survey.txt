Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP: A Survey

Abstract
Large language models store factual knowledge in their parameters, but this parametric knowledge is hard to update and can lead to hallucination. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) combines a parametric language model with a non-parametric retriever over an external corpus. At inference time the retriever fetches relevant documents and the generator conditions on them to produce grounded, citable answers. This survey reviews the core architecture, training strategies, and evaluation of RAG systems.

1 Introduction
Knowledge-intensive tasks such as open-domain question answering require access to a large body of world knowledge. Purely parametric models must memorize all facts during pre-training, which is expensive and brittle, and updating a fact requires retraining or fine-tuning. RAG addresses this by decoupling knowledge storage from the model: documents live in an external index that can be updated at any time without touching model weights. This improves factual accuracy, supports fresh information, and provides citations for transparency.

2 Method
A RAG pipeline has three stages: retrieval, reranking, and generation. The retriever encodes the query and documents into dense vectors and returns the top-k nearest neighbors by cosine similarity. An optional reranker, often a cross-encoder, reorders the candidates for higher precision before they reach the generator. The generator, a sequence-to-sequence or decoder-only language model, conditions on the retrieved passages together with the query to produce the final answer. Recent agentic variants let the model itself decide when to retrieve, how to rewrite the query, and whether to perform multiple retrieval hops, a strategy known as corrective or self-reflective RAG.

3 Experiments
We evaluate RAG on open-domain QA benchmarks including Natural Questions and TriviaQA. Metrics include exact match for answers and recall@k and mean reciprocal rank for retrieval quality. RAG consistently outperforms closed-book baselines that rely only on parametric memory. Adding a cross-encoder reranker further improves answer accuracy, and multi-hop retrieval helps on questions that require reasoning over several documents.

4 Conclusion
RAG is an effective paradigm for grounding language models in external knowledge. Future directions include corrective retrieval, self-reflection over retrieved evidence, and tighter end-to-end integration between the retriever and the generator.
