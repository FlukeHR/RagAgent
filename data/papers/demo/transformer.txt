Attention Is All You Need: The Transformer Architecture

Abstract
The Transformer is a neural network architecture based entirely on attention mechanisms, dispensing with recurrence and convolutions. Self-attention relates different positions of a single sequence in order to compute a representation of that sequence. The Transformer enables significantly more parallelization than recurrent models and achieves strong results on machine translation.

1 Introduction
Recurrent neural networks process sequences step by step, which prevents parallelization within a sequence and makes learning long-range dependencies difficult. The Transformer replaces recurrence with self-attention, allowing every position to attend to every other position with a constant path length, which makes long-range dependencies easier to learn.

2 Method
The core component is scaled dot-product attention. Given queries, keys, and values, attention computes a weighted sum of the values, where the weight assigned to each value is the dot product of the query with the corresponding key, scaled by the square root of the key dimension and normalized with a softmax. Multi-head attention runs several attention functions in parallel and concatenates their outputs, letting the model attend to information from different representation subspaces. The encoder and decoder are stacks of such attention layers interleaved with position-wise feed-forward networks, residual connections, and layer normalization.

3 Experiments
The Transformer is evaluated on WMT English-to-German and English-to-French translation. It achieves higher BLEU scores than previous recurrent and convolutional models while training substantially faster thanks to parallelization. Ablation studies show that the number of attention heads and the model dimension both affect translation quality.

4 Conclusion
Attention-based architectures are powerful and efficient sequence models. The Transformer has become the foundation for modern large language models, including the parametric generators used inside retrieval-augmented generation systems.
